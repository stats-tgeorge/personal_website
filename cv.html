Central Michigan University

PhD Dissertation Research (Advisors: D. Wang and M. Amezziane) (Jan 2016 – Present)

  • Created two new linear regression lack of fit tests specialized in detecting an array of departures from linearity
  • Use power simulations and non-central distribution approximations to find the statistical power of the tests
  • Worked on developing an R programming package LOFnorep containing partition lack of fit methods

Data Analytics Class Project (Professor: C. Lee) (Jan 2017 – May 2017)

  • Found and cleaned data containing every documented crime in New York City, New York, recorded via a New York State database
  • Unsupervised techniques were used to study the relationship between the number of crimes, the types of crimes, and demographics by geographic location within the city (down to census tract)
  • Various predictive models, including linear regression, logistic regression, and neural networks, were fit using cross-validation
  • The chosen model was then scored with the most recent crime data, and the model showed that such predictions can be made with the few demographic variables chosen and location

Graduate Research (Advisor: D. Wang) (Jan 2016 – July 2016)

  • Programmed linear regression lack of fit tests into R to make them accessible to more statisticians and simulated new and past results
  • Hypothesized application of recommended alterations for sinusoidal data structures
